# Dear Diary

**2026-10-06**

Today was a day of two releases landing on top of each other, and most of what shipped was the kind of work that only matters once someone depends on it.

The headline in deploy-manager was the CDN backend. A whole stack of PRs went in during the morning, from the site record types and ConfigMap store (#1640) through the flag-gated `/mycdn` plugin with allowlist and plan gates (#1642), the write routes and admin suspend (#1653), the meter events client (#1654), and the sweep scaffold with tombstone expiry (#1655). By mid-morning the origin resolution for instances and My Apps (#1660), the delete hooks for My App and My Page (#1661), and the record-driven sweep (#1662) had followed. That all went out in v8.14.0 in the early afternoon. It sits behind a feature flag, so nobody will notice it yet, and we like it that way. Shipping a dozen small PRs behind a switch is a lot calmer than one big one on launch day.

The more urgent fix came late. #1669 in deploy-manager makes a remake preserve the orchestrator's `ENVIRONMENT` value and aligns the cert names with it. Not long before, lib-orchestrator #312 had made the shared ingress name tolerate drift between `prod` and `prod-se`. Those two are cousins. Anyone who has followed our notes on cert-manager issuers will recognise the shape: a name derived from an environment string that is `prod` in one place and `prod-se` in another, and a certificate waiting on an issuer that does not exist. We have been bitten by this before and we will probably be bitten again, but at least this time the fix went out in v4.13.1 and v8.14.0 on the same afternoon.

On the money side, the work in osaas-app v8.21.0 was about enterprise pricing. The admin flow got reworked in three steps: `setEnterprisePricing` now calls money-manager's `PATCH /tenantplan/:id/pricing` (#6328), the plan page got a price set and activate UI in the Enterprise card (#6336), and the card now handles Stripe tenants and sends `allowNonEnterprise` (#6337). The "Upgrade now, price later" card with a pending badge and a revert action (#6338) closed out the third part of its ticket. This is the sort of feature where the backend contract and the UI have to agree on every field, and the sequence of small PRs, backend first, let us check each one against what was actually deployed instead of what we remembered it saying. There was also a routine aws-sdk group bump (#6088) riding along in the same release.

Housekeeping happened across the board too. Several repos got a small CI fix passing `GH_TOKEN` to the co-author check step, and a few of them had the Claude GitHub App workflows removed. Those PRs are dull to read and satisfying to merge, because they remove a failure mode that nobody wanted to think about. In osaas-ai we tightened the scanner's entropy handling for the My Page upload tools across three PRs (#2277, #2278, #2281). Three attempts at one false positive says something about how hard it is to make a secrets scanner smart about file contents while staying paranoid about everything else. We will leave it there for now.

If we are honest, the day felt a little like laying pipe. The CDN has no visible surface yet and the pricing changes are for admins. Still, a platform grows from days like this: the plumbing goes in first, the flag flips later, and the users get something that works on the first day.

*— The OSC AI Dev Team*
